## 4. What it costs

We priced the final agent using the three-layer cost-to-serve model required in D6. The frozen evaluation set contained 30 cases: 15 instructor-provided cases, 8 contributed by Fan Yupei, 6 contributed by Liu Xuanlin, and 1 D4 integration case. Because ordinary cases ran once and negative cases ran three times, each final-v2 model produced 58 trials. We used the API-reported token counts from those live runs rather than scripted estimates.

For Problem B, the brief gives a default monthly volume of 4,000 referrals and a failure cost based on a triage nurse earning US$55 per hour and spending 10 minutes on an escalation. This gives US$9.17 per failed task. Layer 1 is the model token cost per referral. Layer 2 is (1 − success rate) × US$9.17. The brief does not give a numeric Layer-3 fixed monthly amount, so we keep US$150 as an explicit team scenario assumption for monitoring and maintenance rather than presenting it as observed hospital data.

The five measured code-pass rates were 43.1% for GPT-5.4, 25.9% for Kimi K2.6, 17.2% for Claude Sonnet 4.6, 10.3% for Gemini 3.7 Flash, and 6.9% for DeepSeek V4 Pro. Under the stated assumptions, the estimated monthly totals are US$21,072.84, US$27,356.66, US$30,556.90, US$33,034.97, and US$34,302.66 respectively. The API price differences are small compared with the fallback layer. For example, Gemini has the lowest model-only cost per task, about US$0.0029, but its low measured success rate creates a much larger expected human-escalation cost.

The ledger also shows that cost and reliability can move in opposite directions. In the controlled descriptor experiment, GPT-5.4 improved from 20/58 to 25/58 code passes, while total API tokens increased from 244,149 to 297,983 and exact price-book cost rose from US$0.7310 to US$0.8812. This was a reliability gain, not a cost saving, and it shows why token cost alone is not enough to judge an interface change.

The ±10 percentage-point sensitivity confirms that success rate is the dominant cost lever. For models away from the 0% boundary, improving success by 10 percentage points reduces expected monthly fallback cost by about US$3,666.67 at 4,000 referrals. We also tested the brief’s break-even question using GPT-5.4 as the higher-performing reference. Gemini, the cheapest model-only option, would need about 43.0% success to break even with GPT-5.4, but it measured 10.3%. Kimi and DeepSeek also fall well below their roughly 43% break-even rates. In this referral task, reducing failures matters much more than choosing the lowest token price.
